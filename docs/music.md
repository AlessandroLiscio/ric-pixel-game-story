# Music and the rhythm game

The nine tracks live in `src/game/audio/music/<id>.ts`, each a `Track` (see `docs/audio-party.md` for the format) built with the small helper `src/game/audio/music/lib/compose.ts` (in `lib/` so the engine's `./music/*.ts` glob never loads it). A track is a list of sections; each section gives `chords` (bars separated by `|`, one chord per bar or two or four, e.g. `F G`), `lead` (the melody, bars separated by `|`, tokens `Name[:steps]` where a bare `E5` lasts 2 steps = an eighth, `E5:4` is a quarter, `-:4` a rest, `=:4` holds the previous note), and the names of a bass, arpeggio and drum pattern from the libraries at the top of `compose.ts` (`BASS`, `ARP`, `DRUMS`) or 16 raw tokens. Bass notes follow the chord root of each step and the arpeggio uses the chord tones, so only the lead can clash; every bar must add up to 16 steps or the module throws when it loads. The result has four channels: lead (square, volume 1), arpeggio (pulse25, 0.5), bass (triangle, 1) and noise drums (0.3). The sequencer scales a channel volume by 0.2, so these give peaks of about 0.2, 0.1, 0.2 and 0.06 and a whole mix peaks around 0.5, well under clipping. Pitched voices have a small ADSR (5 ms attack, 30 ms decay to 70 percent of the peak, a hold, then a release of at most 50 ms at the end of the note; the triangle bass holds at 100 percent), so a `=` hold really sustains; noise drums keep a short exponential decay. Sustain raises the average level (RMS) to about 2.7 times that of the old plucks while the peaks stay the same, so the engine's music bus gain is 0.4.

Retuning: change `bpm` in the track file for speed, edit a `lead` string for the tune (keep each bar at 16 steps), swap `bass`, `arp` or `drums` for another pattern from `compose.ts`, or add a pattern there. After any change run `node scripts/check-music.mjs`: it checks the shape of all nine tracks, that each stays in its key (at most 10 percent out-of-key notes; the key of each track is in the `KEYS` map at the top of the script), that each renders one loop without clipping and that the rhythm chart is sound. Duration, peak, RMS and out-of-key share per track go to `logs/check-music/details.log`. Agents cannot hear, so a human should listen to each track once.

title: heroic and cheerful fanfare at 128 bpm in C major, 24 bars as A B A' (8 bars each): A is a rising C-G-Am-F theme, B is a more lyrical Am-Em-F-C-Am-Dm-G answer, A' repeats A with a big ending; octave-jumping bass, 16th arpeggio, four-on-the-floor drums with a fill every 8 bars.

universita: academic and quirky at 108 bpm in G major, 24 bars as A B A' (8 bars each): short staccato notes with rests between them (pizzicato feel), root-fifth bass, a plucked arpeggio and a metronome tick on the hat; B turns to Am and D with dotted staccato notes.

giuggiu: lazy lounge at 88 bpm in F major, 24 bars as A B A' (8 bars each): swing is written into the notes as long-short pairs (3+1 steps), the chords are jazzy sevenths (Fmaj7, Dm7, Gm7, C7, Am7, Bbmaj7), the bass is sparse, the arpeggio comps on the offbeats and the drums are a soft brush pattern.

giardini: sunny and bouncy at 120 bpm in D major, 24 bars as A B A' (8 bars each): a skipping dotted rhythm (3+1 steps) in the lead and a bouncing bass on the same pulse, D-G-D-A harmony in A and Bm-G-D-A in B.

circe: hurried and comic chase at 140 bpm in A minor (G# as the leading tone), 24 bars as A B A' (8 bars each): running eighths and scale bursts, a driving eighth-note bass, a restless arpeggio and snare-heavy drums; B moves to F-G-Am-Em with sixteenth flourishes.

cortile: warm band-rehearsal rock-pop at 112 bpm in A major, 24 bars as verse, chorus, verse (8 bars each): verse over A-E-F#m-D with a driving syncopated bass and offbeat chord stabs, chorus over D-A-E-F#m with plain eighth bass and an arpeggio, a bigger kit fill at the end of each section.

divano: slow sleepy music-box lullaby at 72 bpm in Bb major, 16 bars as A A' B A (4 bars each): quarter-note melody over Bb-Eb-Bb/F-Bb, two bass notes per bar, a slow arpeggio and a faint hat tick; B visits Gm-Cm-F7.

credits: triumphant and nostalgic at 100 bpm in C major, 24 bars: an 8 bar theme over C-G-Am-F, an 8 bar bigger section, then Happy Birthday as the last phrase (bars 17 to 24, with the pickup notes in the last beat of bar 16) over a gentle accompaniment and an empty last bar; the melody of Happy Birthday is in the public domain (US 2016 judgment, EU term expired), see the comment in the file.

rhythm: the song of the rhythm game at 120 bpm in C major, 14 bars (28 seconds): two intro bars with a silent lead and a steady kick and snare, then A, B (4 bars each) with a catchy eighth-note lead on channel 0, and a 4 bar bass solo to close, where the lead drops to octaves 2 and 3 and walks the chords in eighths while the bass and arpeggio rest; the chart of the minigame is built from this lead, so edit it with care.

The chart (`src/game/minigames/rhythm/chart.ts`) is generated from channel 0 of the rhythm track, once through the song: every lead note that starts on a beat becomes a falling note (the eighths between the beats are melody only, which keeps it playable for a casual player; `BEATS_ONLY` turns that off), and its pitch class picks the lane, C to lane 0 (Q), D and E to lane 1 (W), F and G to lane 2 (E), A and B to lane 3 (R), so a rising melody moves to the right. A note is dropped when it would be less than 0.26 seconds after another on the same lane or would be a third simultaneous note (the check requires zero dropped notes), so with the current tune the chart has 61 notes, 4 seconds to 27 seconds, spaced at least 0.5 seconds on a lane. Lead notes in octave 3 or lower are the bass solo (`SOLO_MAX_OCTAVE`): there every eighth is a chart note, twice the usual density, so consecutive solo notes must change lane; the game shows "ASSOLO DI BASSO!" as the solo's first notes start to fall, and check-music requires the solo to hold at least 16 notes and to come after every other note.

The scene is `src/game/minigames/rhythm/index.ts`; its tuning constants are at the top: hit windows `PERFECT` 0.06 s and `GOOD` 0.12 s, a press within `EARLY` 0.26 s of a note but outside GOOD counts that note as missed once (no double penalty), approval gains `GAIN` (+0.7 perfect, +0.5 good, -1.25 miss or wrong key) from `START_APPROVAL` 50, `FALL_SECONDS` 1.6 for the note speed, `COUNT_STEP` for the 3-2-1 countdown, `AUDIO_LATENCY` as an extra manual offset for the judging (it starts at 0; the beat clock already subtracts the output latency the browser reports). The gains were chosen with a scripted player on the 91-note chart: perfect play reaches 100 at about 40 s of the 52 s song, a player who hits 7 notes in 10 (half of them perfect) ends with 50 or more about three times in four, and a player who presses nothing loses after about 24 s. The game ends when the bar reaches 100 (win), 0 (lose) or when the song ends (win with 50 or more), shows the win or lose text for 1.5 seconds, then emits `minigame:done` and stops its scene and the music. On the debug keys (`debug:minigame`) it only stops itself: the flow emits the result. If the audio clock does not run (a blocked context) the game falls back to its own wall clock, so it stays playable in silence.
