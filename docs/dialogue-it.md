# Dialogue (Italian) for the Ric game

All player-visible text lives in `src/data/dialogue.it.json`, written in Italian and following the dialogue contract (`ui`, `menu`, `controls`, `diary`, `scenes`, `minigames`, `easterEggs`, `credits`, `titleScreen`). Check it with `node scripts/check-dialogue.mjs`: it prints `PASS dialogue: <N> lines, all within limits` or the first 20 problems.

## Structure

A Line is `{ "speaker", "text", "pause"?, "event"?, "laugh"? }`. `event` marks the line of a scene's `meet` after which a happening plays; the one value is `losco` (in `scenes.cortile.meet`: the hooded man walks up to Lara, once). `laugh: true` marks the one line of `scenes.divano` the whole room laughs at in the credits. One Line is one box page: the box wraps the text at its full width (a `\n` in old lines counts as a space), and the validator measures the text with the font's own glyph widths and allows at most 3 wrapped rows. Write a line as one sentence or two, without line breaks. Since round 5 only Giuggiu speaks in dialect: Roman ("Aò", "scialla", "a' zì", "me sto a taglià"), and he alone says "trellone" (everyone else, narrator, menu and credits included, says canna, cannetta or cannone). Everybody else speaks plain Italian with at most a small mark: Ric from Bari ("Trimò" for his friends, "Meh", "Sciamanin", and no Bari sayings: he never used them), Lollino Tuscan only in a word such as "Maremma" (never the gorgia written as `h`), Liscio with no regional spelling, Ros, Giusy (half sentences, "vabè, capisci!", "daje") and Lara (super excited about playing). Swearing is fine. No line mentions a grandmother. The validator enforces the "trellone" rule, the grandmother rule and Lollino's spelling. Speakers are the personIds (`ric`, `lara`, `liscio`, `ros`, `lollino`, `fede`, `giuggiu`, `giusy`, `pinolo`, `gatto`, `sconosciuta`) or `narratore`. The only placeholder is `{ric}`; the validator counts the raw text, so the real length after substitution is shorter.

Story rules after the owner's playtest, all enforced by the validator. The story is an ordinary Friday in Bologna, never Ric's birthday: no birthday word inside `scenes`, `easterEggs` or `minigames` (Prof. Pinolo's "trent'anni" of pinhole cameras is not one); only the title screen (`titleScreen.subtitle`), the credits title and the last credits line, "Buon compleanno, Ric!" (shown big by the finale), stay as the real gift message, because they sit outside the story. Every character name written in a text starts with a capital letter (Ric, Lollino, Pinolo, Giuggiu, Liscio, Ros, Giusy, Lara, Fede, Gatto di Paz); the house cat is always "Gatto di Paz" (speaker id `gatto`), never "il gatto". The slackline is "slack", never "fettuccia". Characters never explain a minigame: the rules live only in `minigames.<id>.title` and `.instructions`, shown by the instruction card, and the lines of `minigameIntro*` are friends talking. Prof. Pinolo's lessons take place at the Cinema Bellinzona (red velvet seats in rows, a big screen, wall lights). The `join` of every scene with a next destination names that destination and ends with the line `ric` "Meh, sciamanin." (the last scene, `divano`, has no next destination and is exempt); the flow plays `join` last, so that line closes the scene's story before the join banner.

`scenes` holds the six sceneIds in order, each with `arrival`, `meet`, `minigameIntro`, `win`, `lose` and `join`. The flow plays arrival, meet, minigameIntro, the minigame, then win or lose, then join (the friend joins the party in both cases). Every scene path is about 8 to 15 pages.

Deviations from the contract, both needed by the story and listed here for the flow worker. First, `scenes.circe` also has `minigameIntro2`, `win2` and `lose2` for the second minigame of the scene: `minigameIntro`, `win` and `lose` belong to the d20 (Ros) and the `*2` keys to mash (Giusy); `join` closes the scene and covers Ros and Giusy together. Second, `scenes.divano` has no minigame, so `minigameIntro`, `win` and `lose` are empty arrays; `arrival`, `meet` and `join` are spoken during the credits, each line under 110 characters. `meet` is the TV exchange: the fish documentary praises its creatures, the line before the last is the narrator's and ends "... le acciughe!", and the last one is the laugh (`"laugh": true`), which the whole room acts out. The validator enforces both: exactly one laugh line, right after the anchovies.

`minigames.<id>` has `title` (max 32 characters), `instructions` (max 2 rows of 44), `winText` and `loseText` (max 2 rows of 44, a short result card). `easterEggs` holds the ten eggs plus the portal gun lines (`portalFound`, `binEmpty` where each entry is one variant picked at random, and `portalNotYet`, the one fixed line Ric says on P before the scene is done); `menu`, `controls` and `diary` (one entry per scene id, up to 4 rows of 44) hold the text of the Esc menu; `credits` and `titleScreen` follow the contract. `ui` has the keys the contract names plus a few extras (`confirm`, `fastForward`, `talk`, `look`, `run`, `menu`, `exit` (the hint "Esci dalla stanza"), `loading`, `joined`, `theEnd`) that the UI worker may use or ignore.

## Easter egg triggers (assumed)

No minigame result changes the credits: the upstairs gag (`ending.upstairs`, or `ending.upstairsFrangetta` when Ric got the fringe girl's number) plays every time. `loscoApproach` is the narrator line while the hooded man walks up to Lara, `losco` his offer. The cinema has four look-at spots besides the screen and the seats (`objects.universita`: `notice`, `speaker`, `exitdoor`, `sleeper`). `fede` is a joke in the cortile scene, not an NPC (author decision): the generator still draws a Fede sheet and portrait (used in the title and credits), but no scene places him, and `scenes.cortile` has no Fede line. After the stage with Lara, Ric can ask her; the egg is exactly 2 Lines, `ric` "Allora, Fede dov'è?" and `lara` "Ha paccato.", and the validator enforces both texts. It is always available, not tied to a win.
## Extra NPC lines the author can swap

`extra-3` is my own invention: the barista Alessandro, who offers Ric a drink at the cortile. The two earlier inventions (a student in the lecture hall and a dog walker) are gone. Swap its lines freely, keeping the id (the validator expects it).

## Assumptions

- Working title: `Ric 30: Un Venerdì a Bologna`. Subtitle and press text as briefed.
- Genders: Ric and Prof. Pinolo are male, Giusy is female (the brief says "her seat"). For everyone else the lines avoid gendered forms.
- "Trellone" is Giuggiu's own word; smoking is always a light joke, never an instruction or praise; the friends' personalities are light inventions: Lollino studious (notes and colour pens), Giuggiu the chill host, Liscio the sporty acrobat, Ros the sarcastic rebel, Giusy always late, Lara the musical one.
- The cat is named Gatto di Paz (speaker id `gatto`, name in `people.json`). It only says `Miao.` with a parenthetical translation in some lines.
- The two cortile guitarists and the drummer are never named.
- A flat path through the game is about 100 dialogue pages; with six minigames (about 1 minute each) the run is about 20 minutes.

## Line counts

The validator's PASS line gives the total. A single run shows about 8 to 15 Lines per scene (win and lose are alternatives).
